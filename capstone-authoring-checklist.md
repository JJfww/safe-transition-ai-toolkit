# SAFE Transition AI Toolkit Authoring Checklist

This checklist is the working plan for creating the published capstone materials. The core constraint is that the final public writing should be yours. AI can help refine, organize, check clarity, or suggest options, but each published page, tool, guide, checklist, and prompt should begin from your own sketch, notes, outline, or draft.

## Authoring Rule for Every Published Part

- [ ] Write a first-pass sketch in your own words before using AI support.
- [ ] Mark the intended audience for the piece: transition educator, job coach, paraprofessional, program leader, family, or student-facing support.
- [ ] Identify the real transition problem the piece solves.
- [ ] Note what should never be delegated to AI in that context.
- [ ] Use AI only to refine structure, clarity, examples, wording options, accessibility, or consistency.
- [ ] Review every AI-refined passage for accuracy, tone, bias, privacy, and alignment with transition practice.
- [ ] Revise the final version manually before publication.
- [ ] Keep a record of major sources, frameworks, or district/professional guidance used.
- [ ] Confirm that no identifiable student, family, staff, employer-sensitive, IEP, disability, or assessment information appears in examples.
- [ ] Confirm that final text keeps student voice, privacy, accessibility, and human judgment central.

## First-Principles Text Review

Use this pass on every page draft, callout, checklist, prompt block, and tool instruction before publication.

- [ ] Name the core truth the section must communicate in one sentence.
- [ ] State the real staff problem this section helps solve.
- [ ] Remove any sentence that sounds general but does not help staff act.
- [ ] Separate durable principles from habits, assumptions, or filler wording.
- [ ] Check that the section says what AI can help with in this specific context.
- [ ] Check that the section says what a human still has to decide, review, notice, or protect.
- [ ] Check that the section says what information should never be entered casually into AI tools.
- [ ] Replace generic school-AI wording with transition-specific language, examples, or decisions.
- [ ] Check whether the section protects student voice, privacy, and dignity in concrete terms rather than slogans.
- [ ] Rewrite any overclaim so the language stays accurate, bounded, and defensible.

### First-Principles Prompt

Use these questions when drafting or revising:

- [ ] What is the irreducible point this section needs to make?
- [ ] What problem is the educator actually facing here?
- [ ] What must remain true even if the AI tool changes?
- [ ] What part of this section is convention or filler rather than substance?
- [ ] Where must human judgment remain fully in control?
