# About / Capstone Rationale

Status: draft

This capstone focuses on transition education because the work is deeply personal, individualized, and future-facing. Staff often need practical support with communication, instruction, and employment preparation, but those supports must not override student voice, privacy protections, or IEP team judgment.

The toolkit uses a static-site model and a browser-based prompt builder rather than a live AI integration. This website is not an AI tool: nothing entered here is sent to Gemini, ChatGPT, or another AI service. The lab runs in the browser, does not save text, and creates a structured prompt only. An outside AI tool is involved only if a user chooses to copy that prompt and paste it there.

## What this page helps staff do

This page explains why the project is scoped the way it is. It helps readers understand that the capstone is intentionally specific, practical, and cautious rather than broad, flashy, or product-driven.

## What still requires human review

- Whether the toolkit language remains grounded in real transition practice
- Whether any example or prompt overclaims what AI can do responsibly
- Whether the final public version still sounds like a professional transition resource rather than a generic AI site

## Information staff should not enter casually

The site itself does not ask for student information, but the same privacy expectations apply when users leave the site and work in external tools.

## About the author

### Jennifer Burnes, PhD

Jennifer Burnes, PhD, is a Transition Specialist with Lawrence Public Schools' Community Transition Program. She develops and implements transition programming for young adults ages 18-21 with disabilities, with a focus on employment, independent living, self-advocacy, and community participation.

Her work includes building partnerships with employers and community agencies, coordinating services with students, families, and school teams, and training job coaches to support student independence. She also provides education and technical assistance that help adult learners and transition professionals turn complex information into practical tools, training, and support.

Before her career in education, Jennifer worked in financial-services analytics, building automated risk-monitoring processes and using data to inform customer-acquisition decisions. For more than 20 years, she has applied that systems-minded approach to instruction, research, program evaluation, and data-informed practice, finding practical ways to make systems more useful for educators and expand access, independence, and opportunity for young adults with disabilities.

Jennifer values teaching young adults to understand and communicate their strengths, interests, and preferences as they prepare for self-advocacy, employment, and independent living. She also enjoys helping educators and related-service professionals understand assessment and data, then use it effectively to improve outcomes.
