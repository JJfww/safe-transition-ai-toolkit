# About / Capstone Rationale

Status: draft

This capstone focuses on transition education because the work is deeply personal, individualized, and future-facing. Staff often need practical support with communication, instruction, and employment preparation, but those supports must not override student voice, privacy protections, or IEP team judgment.

The toolkit uses a static-site model and a browser-based prompt builder rather than a live AI integration. This website is not an AI tool: nothing entered here is sent to Gemini, ChatGPT, or another AI service. The lab runs in the browser, does not save text, and creates a structured prompt only. An outside AI tool is involved only if a user chooses to copy that prompt and paste it there.

## What this page helps staff do

This page explains why the project is scoped the way it is. It helps readers understand that the capstone is intentionally specific, practical, and cautious rather than broad, flashy, or product-driven.

## What still requires human review

- Whether the toolkit language remains grounded in real transition practice
- Whether any example or prompt overclaims what AI can do responsibly
- Whether the final public version still sounds like a professional transition resource rather than a generic AI site

## Information staff should not enter casually

The site itself does not ask for student information, but the same privacy expectations apply when users leave the site and work in external tools.

## About the author

### Jennifer Burnes, PhD

Jennifer Burnes, PhD, is an educator and problem-solver with a background in analytical work. Before her career in education, she worked in financial-services analytics, building automated risk-monitoring processes and using data to inform customer-acquisition decisions. For more than 20 years, she has applied that systems-minded approach to finding practical solutions, providing education and technical assistance that make systems more useful for educators and expand access, independence, and opportunity for young adults with disabilities.

One of her proudest teaching moments came when a graduate student shared that, for the first time, she could explain complicated assessment information to a student's parents with confidence and accuracy rather than simply glossing over it.
