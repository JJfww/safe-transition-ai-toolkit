# Start Here: Responsible AI in Transition

Status: draft

AI can support planning, communication, accessibility, and instruction in transition work, but it should not make decisions about students, define their futures, or speak for them.

## What this page helps staff do

This page establishes the working stance for the entire toolkit. It helps staff separate helpful support uses from uses that could weaken privacy, professional responsibility, or student voice.

## Core stance

- AI is a support tool, not a decision-maker.
- Student voice should remain central.
- Identifiable student information must be protected.
- Human review is required.
- AI outputs must be checked for bias, assumptions, accuracy, and respectful language.
- Transition planning must remain individualized.

## What still requires human review

- Whether the output reflects the actual student, workplace, or instructional context
- Whether the response includes stereotypes, overstatements, or missing nuance
- Whether the language is respectful and useful for transition practice
- Whether the proposed use shifts too much authority away from educators, students, or teams

## Information staff should not enter casually

- Any direct student identifiers
- IEP content or disability documentation
- Private family information
- Sensitive employer information
- Raw student data pasted into general-purpose tools without a stronger review process

## Use the minimum needed

Remove names and identifying details whenever possible. If your district has approved a specific AI tool for this kind of work, follow its data-use rules and your district's privacy guidance. District approval is not blanket permission to enter every detail from a student record.

## Appropriate use example

An educator can ask AI to rewrite a workplace expectation in plainer language, then review the response and adapt it for a specific student and setting.

## Inappropriate use example

An educator should not ask AI to decide whether a student is ready for a job placement or to produce final IEP recommendations without human judgment and team review.
