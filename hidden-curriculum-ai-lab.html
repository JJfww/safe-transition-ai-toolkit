AI Drafting Lab for Transition and Special Education Work

Use this page as a drafting hub for three kinds of transition work: IEP development, transition plan development, and hidden curriculum development. The interactive tool now supports all three areas while keeping their review boundaries visible.

What this page helps staff do

Use AI as a careful drafting support while keeping student voice, privacy, human review, and professional judgment central.

Three drafting lanes

These sections do not carry the same level of risk. Hidden curriculum drafting is narrower and more instruction-focused. IEP and transition plan drafting require stronger review and much tighter privacy boundaries.

IEP development

Use AI carefully for drafting goal language, organizing notes, and summarizing present levels after removing identifying information.

Transition plan development

Use AI carefully to analyze transition assessment themes, brainstorm possible postsecondary goals, and draft services or annual goals for review.

Hidden curriculum development

Use AI to surface unwritten expectations, build student-friendly explanations, and strengthen workplace instruction and coaching.

IEP development

Useful for drafting and organizing language, but not for making eligibility, placement, service, or readiness decisions.

  • Possible uses: writing goal options, summarizing present levels, organizing source notes.
  • Human review required: accuracy, individualization, compliance, tone, and whether the draft reflects actual student data.
  • Do not paste casually: names, full IEP text, protected records, identifying assessment details, or private family information.

Transition plan development

Useful for pattern-finding and drafting options, but not for deciding the student’s future, goals, or services.

  • Possible uses: analyzing transition assessment data, suggesting postsecondary goal options, brainstorming transition services, drafting annual transition goals.
  • Human review required: alignment with assessment evidence, student preferences, feasibility, dignity, and team decision-making.
  • Do not paste casually: identifiable assessment reports, disability-specific narratives, or employer or family-private details.

Higher-review reminder

IEP and transition plan drafting are more sensitive than hidden curriculum drafting. Use the minimum information needed, treat every output as provisional, and keep final decisions with educators, students, families, and the full team. If your district has approved a specific AI tool for this work, follow its data-use rules; approval is not blanket permission to paste every detail from a student record.

Interactive drafting tool

Choose the drafting area first, then generate a more specific prompt for the task you actually need. The tool stays client-side only and does not send text anywhere by itself.

IEP source text

De-identified present-level notes, skill summaries, observation notes, or draft language that needs clearer organization.

Transition plan source text

De-identified transition assessment notes, student interest summaries, strengths, needs, and planning notes for goals or services.

Hidden curriculum source text

Job descriptions, handbook excerpts, short rules, task lists, training language, or supervisor expectations.

Use the lab as a careful drafting tool.

The lab does not interpret the student or make decisions. It gives staff a more deliberate structure for turning source text into clearer, reviewable prompt language across all three drafting areas.
